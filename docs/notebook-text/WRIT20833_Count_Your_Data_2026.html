def split_into_words(any_chunk_of_text):
    lowercase_text = any_chunk_of_text.lower()
    # split on any run of non-letter/number characters (spaces, punctuation, newlines)
    return re.split(r"\W+", lowercase_text)

def top_meaningful_words(text, n):
    meaningful_words = []
    for word in split_into_words(text):
        if word != "" and word not in stopwords:
            meaningful_words.append(word)
    return Counter(meaningful_words).most_common(n)

Step 4: Count Every Comment at Once

topmeaningfulwords takes one string. Your comments are a whole column of strings. So first, glue the column into one long text, the same way String Methods glued a list of words back together. Step 6: Words or People?

A count says how many times a word appears, not how many people used it. One commenter who writes "love love LOVE" adds three. So for each top word, check how many comments contain it at all. This uses the filter from Pandas 02: mydf[mydf["columnname"].str.lower().str.contains("word", regex=False)] keeps only the rows whose text includes that word, and len(...) counts those rows. One honest limit: .str.contains("love") also matches "glove" and "lovely." It's a quick check, not an exact one.

# READ THIS ONE: for each top word, times used vs. comments that use it
lowercase_text = my_df[MY_TEXT_COLUMN].str.lower()

print(f"{'word':>14}  {'times':>6}  {'comments':>8}")
for word, count in top_words[:10]:
    comments_with_word = my_df[lowercase_text.str.contains(word, regex=False)]
    print(f"{word:>14}  {count:6}  {len(comments_with_word):8}")

print()
print("Total comments:", len(my_df))

Where the two numbers are far apart, a few people are repeating themselves (or one comment is very long). Where they're close, the word is spread across many voices. Which kind are your top words?

Step 8: Predictions on the Record

What VADER is (the short version)

On Monday you'll use VADER, a sentiment analysis tool. Its job is to read a piece of text and say how positive or negative it sounds. Researchers built it for short social media posts like yours. Here's all you need to know for now: - It starts from a word list. People rated thousands of words for feeling, from very negative to very positive. "Love" and "great" score positive, "hate" and "awful" negative, and most words, like "school" or "law," score zero. - It adds a few rules. ALL CAPS and "!!!" make a feeling stronger. "Not" flips it ("not good" reads as negative). "But" gives more weight to what comes after it. - Emoji are hit or miss. VADER swaps each emoji for its name and scores those words. So 😂 ("face with tears of joy") counts as positive and 😢 ("crying face") as negative, but 😡 ("pouting face") and 👍 ("thumbs up") score zero, and 🔥 ("fire") counts as negative. - It gives each comment one score, from -1 (most negative) to +1 (most positive), and labels the comment positive, negative, or neutral. - It reads words, not meaning. It doesn't know who is speaking, what the video was about, or whether someone is joking.
